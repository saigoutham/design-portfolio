# Diwali Festival of Lights: concept

Stage 3, 2026-11-09. Client track: goes to Ira inside the Gate 2 proposal with the working hero and story one, not on its own. Built on research opportunities 1 and 3, with 2 folded into the signature move.

**It's the site where** I carried one small flame from lamp to lamp, and every lamp I lit opened the same night painted by a different part of India.

## Approaches

| | Concept | Trade-off | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The row of lamps.** Six clay lamps in a row on a dark night. Hold to light one and its story opens in its region's art, three panels long; its last panel lights a lamp inside the painting, and that flame is carried to the next lamp in the row. | Six art traditions to draw; the flame must look real in every one of them. | Chosen |
| 2 | **The storyteller's book.** Six chapters with hard cuts between traditions, a folio in the margin, a colophon close. | The most robust and readable, but it reads rather than immerses, and lighting a lamp becomes decoration. | Runner-up |
| 3 | **The night map.** Fly over a dark India as regions light up, then descend into each painting. | One canvas can't switch between six art traditions without seams, it repeats robot-mammoth's grammar, and a drawn map of India carries legal boundary risk. | Rejected |

## Order of the lamps

Default order follows the festival's days, so no story ranks above another and the visitor learns the five days on the way. The row lets the visitor light any lamp first.

| Lamp | Story | When | Where it's told | Drawn after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lakshmi rises from the churned ocean | Dhanteras (in one tradition) | Odisha | Pattachitra |
| 2 | Satyabhama and Krishna defeat Narakasura | Naraka Chaturdashi, before dawn | Andhra Pradesh | Tholu Bommalata |
| 3 | Rama comes home | The new-moon night | Bihar (Mithila) | Madhubani |
| 4 | Mahavira's nirvana | The new-moon night | Gujarat | Jain Kalpasutra painting |
| 5 | Bandi Chhor Divas | The new-moon night | Punjab | Phulkari, the lit Harmandir Sahib |
| 6 | King Bali comes home for a day | Padwa, the next morning | Maharashtra | Dashavatara Ganjifa (Sawantwadi) |

This replaces the brief's numbering, which put Rama first only because he is the best-known.

## Journey

1. **The dark:** a near-black new-moon night, six unlit clay lamps, each with a museum label. One line, and "Hold to light". The visitor knows there are six stories and that they will light them.
2. **Lakshmi (Pattachitra):** the first flame catches and its glow opens onto the churning ocean in fine red-and-ochre linework; Lakshmi rises. The visitor learns that a lamp opens a painted world.
3. **Narakasura (Tholu Bommalata):** the flame leaves the Pattachitra lamp and crosses to lamp 2, and the world changes medium: translucent coloured puppets thrown on a lit screen, a battle before dawn. The visitor understands the system: every lamp is a different art.
4. **Rama (Madhubani):** a city of Madhubani line and pattern lights lamp by lamp for someone coming home. Told as north India tells it.
5. **Mahavira (Kalpasutra):** the quietest lamp. Gold and ultramarine, a still figure, and the kings who say that since the light of knowledge has gone, they will light lamps. Short, and deliberately silent.
6. **Bandi Chhor (Phulkari) — the peak:** 52 hands take hold of 52 tassels and walk out of the fort; across the water the Harmandir Sahib lights up and its reflection doubles it. The Guru is present only as light.
7. **Bali and the row:** Padwa morning in round Ganjifa cards; Bali keeps his word and is welcomed home for a day. Then the worlds fall away and the row stands complete, six lamps lit. A label plate: Deepavali, a row of lamps.

## Grammar: gallery / catalog

The six stories are a collection of equals, so the page is a room of objects with one label schema, not an argument. Every lamp carries the same label: number, story, when, where it's told, drawn after. The schema is the constant that makes six different art styles read as one site.

- **Nav:** the row of six lamps is the index. It stays at the foot of the screen, jumps to any lamp, and keeps each lit lamp lit.
- **Hero:** the collection itself, already in view and labelled. No title stage above it.
- **Spine:** lateral pan. Each story's three panels pass sideways like a painted scroll, and the flame travels sideways along the row between stories.
- **Close:** a final plate set exactly like a label, so the ending reads as part of the collection.
- **Bans honoured:** no kinetic headlines, no kit spotlight, no magnetic button, at most one scrub, no copy scrimmed over paintings (text sits in a label band beside or under each panel), no persuasion in labels.

Why not the others:
- **Filmic one-shot:** forbids an index and jumping; six equal stories are not one linear argument.
- **Chaptered editorial:** the runner-up (concept 2). Its title-page hero bans media above the fold, and it reads rather than immerses.
- **Live surface:** there is no product to operate.
- **Continuous world:** concept 3. One canvas can't hold six traditions seamlessly, and robot-mammoth already uses it.
- **Typographic poster:** the assets are six painting traditions, not a sentence.
- **Split stage:** there are six sides, not two.
- **Rhythmic cutlist:** speed fights lamp-lit pacing; a Jain nirvana at cut speed would be wrong.

## Signature move

**One flame, carried.** The flame is the only thing drawn the same in every world: one procedural flame, one warm colour. Everything around it changes tradition. Each story's last panel lights a lamp inside the painting (a Pattachitra lamp, a puppet torch, a Madhubani diya, the kings' lamps, the Harmandir lights, Bali's welcome lamp); that flame lifts out of the painting, travels along the row, and waits at the next clay lamp until the visitor holds to light it. Lamps on Diwali are lit from one another; here the stories are too.

- **Why only here:** the mechanic is the site's argument. One festival, many stories, one light passing through all of them.
- **Not a spotlight:** it never follows a pointer and never uncovers a hidden image; the light belongs to a lamp the visitor lit.
- **Every input:** press and hold (about 600 ms, a wick catching) by touch, mouse, or Enter/Space. No cursor needed.
- **A reward, not a gate:** scrolling past an unlit lamp lights it automatically after a pause, so nobody gets stuck.
- **Budget:** the hold and the lit row are the page's only responses to the visitor. No pointer parallax.
- **Reduced motion:** lamps light with a fade, panels are stills, every word stays.

## Feeling curve

| Beat | Feeling | What on screen causes it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 The dark | Anticipation | Six unlit lamps and one instruction, nothing else |
| 2 Lakshmi | Wonder | The first flame's glow opens into a painted ocean |
| 3 Narakasura | Tension | The world turns to lit-from-behind puppets mid-battle, before dawn |
| 4 Rama | Warmth | A city lighting lamps one by one for someone coming home |
| 5 Mahavira | Stillness | Almost nothing moves; a light goes out and kings answer it with lamps |
| 6 Bandi Chhor | Elation | 52 hands hold on and walk free; the Harmandir Sahib lights and doubles in the water |
| 7 Bali and the row | Peace | Morning, a king welcomed home, then six lamps steady in a row |

Peak: beat 6, the longest span on the page (about 2.6 viewport-heights), with beat 5 as its silence. Said the way a visitor would tell a friend: "52 hands grabbed the tassels of a cloak and walked out, and then the whole golden temple lit up in the water."

Pacing: hero 1vh, lamps 1.8vh each, Mahavira 1.4vh, Bandi Chhor 2.6vh, close 1vh. About 13vh in all, inside the 8 to 14 budget. One line in second person, at the close.

## Risks for stage 4

- **The flame.** It has to read as real fire beside six flat art styles. Plan: a small fragment shader; fallback: layered SVG flame with a GSAP flicker. No 3D pack needed.
- **The art.** 18 panels in six traditions drawn in code. Stage 4 proves the system on the hero and lamp 1 (Pattachitra, the finest linework) first.
- **Sensitivity.** Lamps 4 and 5 should be read by a Jain and a Sikh reader before launch.
