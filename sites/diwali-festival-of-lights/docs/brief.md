# Diwali Festival of Lights: brief

Track: client (Ira). Status: awaiting Gate 1. Launch: Mon 23 Nov 2026, the eve of Dev Diwali (24 Nov).
Shared with Ira as a doc for approval; this file is the source of truth.

## In one line
An immersive web telling, in English, of the stories behind Diwali, for every age and for people meeting the festival for the first time, with each story drawn in the art tradition of the state that tells it.

## Decided
- Client: Ira, a storyteller of epics.
- Stories: all of them, one lamp each (list below).
- Structure: one night, many lamps. The visitor starts in the dark of the new-moon night; each lamp lit opens one story, told in three scenes.
- Art: each story in its state's tradition, chosen by Claude (table below).
- Assets: none from Ira; everything made from scratch.
- Language: English only; original names and terms kept where a story needs them.
- Ending: quiet. No call to action.
- Deadline: two weeks, launch Mon 23 Nov 2026.
- Priority: quality of the output over scope. A story that isn't good enough by Fri 20 Nov is held back rather than shipped weak (Goutham and Ira decide on the day).

## Claude's proposals (for Ira to approve or change)
- **Who comes first:** a first-time visitor of any age, on a phone. Text short and plain enough to be read aloud to a child.
- **The ending:** when the last lamp is lit, all of them glow together, the night holds for a moment, and the page rests. Sharing happens through the link preview, designed as carefully as the site.
- **How we'll know it worked (90 days):** how many visitors light every lamp, measured without cookies; plus the awards it enters.
- **After launch:** nobody edits it; no CMS; the content is fixed.
- **Accuracy:** Claude writes all six tellings from published sources and lists them; Ira checks every word at the midpoint review.
- **Respect:** no human depiction of the Sikh Gurus (SGPC and Akal Takht prohibit it); the Jain and Sikh stories are framed as other lamps lit on the same night, not as parts of a Hindu epic; each art style credited "after <tradition>", never presented as the craft itself.
- **Art production:** layered vector art drawn in code, following each tradition's line, palette, borders and figure conventions, so every layer can move. No generated imitations of living crafts.

## Not in scope
- Other languages, shop, sign-up, newsletter, comments.
- Invented quotes, statistics or testimonials.
