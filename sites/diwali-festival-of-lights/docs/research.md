## 2. Audience

- Diaspora parents use Diwali to pass on stories, and Rama and Sita come first by default ([HinduTone](https://hindutone.com/festivals/diwali-2026/diwali-in-the-western-world-community-and-culture/)).
- The "many stories" framing is now common in explainers: which story you grew up with depends on where your family is from ([SacredCal](https://sacredcal.com/blog/diwali-2026), [KidsGen](https://www.kidsgen.com/events/diwali/history.htm)).
- Sikhs notice whether Bandi Chhor Divas is named correctly ([SacredCal](https://sacredcal.com/blog/diwali-2026)); the name itself is a statement of independence ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandi_Chhor_Divas)).
- Whether Valmiki mentions lamps for Rama is argued about online ([The Wire](https://thewire.in/religion/the-many-layers-of-diwali)). The site states traditions as traditions and stays out of the argument.

What they all do: say in words that Diwali is many stories, then show it in one visual register; put Rama first and largest; give the Jain and Sikh stories a line or a late section; present each story as a card or text block; end with a promotion or a newsletter. Nobody makes light itself the way the story is told.

## 4. Taste: exact values that make the references work

**MotionSites, Mostar Guide** (`library/motionsites/examples/mostar-guide.md`), a three-screen cinematic scroll for one city:
- Rig: `.cinema-scroll` height `calc(100vh + 3700px)`; `.stage` sticky, `100vh`, `min-height: 620px`, `overflow: hidden`.
- Planes: separate transparent PNG layers (sky, back stack, mid, split frames, bridge foreground), each driven by its own CSS custom property; back stack `scale(0.76)` growing by `progress * 0.2`, origin `50% 100%`.
- Scroll feel: smoothed scroll `lerp(…, 0.14)`, pointer `lerp(…, 0.12)`; each scene is a `smoothstep` enter/exit window in pixels (`560→900` in, `1300→1620` out).
- Type: display serif title `14rem`, line-height `0.78`, stepping to `11rem / 7.5rem / 4.5rem` at breakpoints; panel `h2` `4.75rem` / `0.95`; body `1.18rem` / `1.18`, weight 500, `text-shadow: 0 2px 18px rgba(0,0,0,0.42)` to read over imagery.
- Colour: paper `#fdf1e1` on near-black `#0b1110`. Card motion `640ms cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`.
- Why it works: every plane moves for a reason (near passes far), and the type is huge and few.

**MotionSites, Interactive Discovery** (`interactive-discovery.md`), a dark hero where a light reveals a second image:
- Spotlight radius `260px`, eased follow `+= (target - current) * 0.1` per frame.
- Soft edge from a radial mask: stops `0→1, 0.4→1, 0.6→0.75, 0.75→0.4, 0.88→0.12, 1→0`. The long tail is what makes it read as light rather than a hole.
- Entrance: `1.1s cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` from `translateY(28px)` and `blur(12px)`, lines staggered `0.25s`, `0.42s`.
- Principle to adapt, not copy: light uncovers a painted layer. Ours cannot depend on a cursor: phones first.

**Scroll-craft approved collection** (`library/scroll-craft/references/approved-collection.md`):
- Write a layer contract for the opening (far, mid, focal, near, atmosphere, type) and describe opening, midpoint and exit in plain words before making assets.
- Art-direct the phone separately and check 360 × 640; never shrink the desktop composition.
- Every scene needs a no-WebGL poster and a complete reduced-motion reading.
- Prove it with screenshots of intermediate states, not just start and end.

## Opportunities

1. **Show the plurality instead of saying it.** Every competitor writes "different stories in different homes" and then shows one style. Six living art traditions on one night make the claim visible; the moment one lamp's world gives way to the next tradition is the signature. (Recommended basis for the concept.)
2. **Make light the interface.** The lamp is how the story is uncovered: a soft, flame-shaped falloff (the long-tail mask above) reveals each painted world. On phones the flame is lit by touch and hold; no cursor needed. No competitor uses light to tell the story.
3. **Build the row of lamps.** Deepavali means a row of lamps. Every lamp is equal and the visitor chooses the order; Jain and Sikh lamps stand beside the others, each carrying its own reason for light (the Kalpasutra's kings, the lit Harmandir Sahib). The quiet ending is the row itself, complete.
