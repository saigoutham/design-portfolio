# Concept

Two rounds of art proofs (a 3D melon cut, pure code and owner stills) failed six blind critics: "a cut-open fruit is the reveal cliché; nothing says soda". The council then weighed three concepts:

1. **Glass fill.** A coded glass fills as you scroll; a rail shows the making. Rejected: generic pink soda, no watermelon in it, another photoreal object judged on craft.
2. **Type fill.** The headline is the vessel; soda fills the letters. Rejected: a familiar trope that reads as the masks the owner already rejected.
3. **Flesh submerge (picked, unanimous).** The fruit and the drink in one frame. The owner's cut-face photo fills the screen, relit by shader. Scrolling raises a soda line: below it the flesh is seen through pink soda, bubbles start on the seeds and rise, the headline refracts under the surface, and the liquid sloshes with scroll speed. A trace rail stamps how it was made: Cut, Press, Add sparkling water, Bottle.

**What a visitor tells a friend:** "You scroll and the watermelon goes under soda; the seeds start fizzing."
