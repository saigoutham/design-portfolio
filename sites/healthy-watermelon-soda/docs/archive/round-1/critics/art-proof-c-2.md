# Art proof C-2: W (CG and food-photography lens)

**Sequence:** A macro watermelon cut face with cream headline "Real fruit. Real fizz." gets flooded from the bottom by a pink, bubble-studded liquid layer that rises to about 30% height and submerges the type.

## Scores (start 6)

- **Craft 5/10.**
  - Earned: W-0.3 is a convincing wet macro. Seeds carry specular highlights, the flesh is glistening, the cream type is crisp and legible (strong contrast).
  - Lost: the liquid does not read as liquid. In W-2.5 and W-3.5 the submerged half is the same photo with a pink wash, a slight wobble and a hairline meniscus. There is no real refraction shift, no depth or thickness gradient, no caustics, no foam head and no wet cling or contact line on the fruit above the surface. It reads as a translucent plastic film, not watermelon soda, and a flat pink pastel is less appetising than the fruit above it.
  - Bubbles (W-3.5, W-5) are near-uniform clear spheres with weak highlights and no rise streaks. They are scattered evenly, like stickers, not a fizz column. W-1.5 has a single lonely bubble.
  - W-5: submerged type is pale pink on pink, roughly 1.5:1 contrast. The headline's final resting state is barely legible, with bubbles crossing letterforms.
- **Motion 6/10.**
  - From stills only: the surface rises in clean, monotone steps (y about 940, 600, 350, 310), and the type wobbling and fading as it crosses the surface in W-2.5 and W-3.5 is a good idea.
  - Nothing proves secondary motion (waves, bubble acceleration, meniscus lag). The slow-down to the end state (W-3.5 to W-5, about 40px) looks like a stall.
- **Creativity 6/10.** Immersing the headline in the product ("fruit becomes fizz") is a subject-specific idea, partly carried through (W-2.5). It is literal and well-trodden, and the end state is not a memorable signature frame.

## Top fixes

1. **W-2.5 / W-3.5 (liquid body).** The liquid is a tint. Add real refraction (offset and magnify the fruit below the surface), a depth gradient (darker and more saturated at the bottom), a bright fizzing foam or meniscus band with contact highlights on the fruit above, and small light caustics. Push the colour toward juicy red-pink, not pastel.
2. **W-5 (type and end state).** Submerged type is washed out. Keep cream text at 4.5:1 or better through the liquid (separate type layer with its own refraction, or hold the headline above the final waterline). Let the surface settle lower or higher so it does not slice the headline's x-height.
3. **W-3.5 / W-5 (bubbles).** Use varied sizes with depth blur, rising streams from seeds and flesh nucleation points, bright rim highlights, and a few breaking at the surface with micro-splashes. Remove the even scatter.

Would a viewer believe the fruit? Yes. The liquid? Not yet. Want to drink it? No, it looks like a pink filter.

VERDICT: FAIL
